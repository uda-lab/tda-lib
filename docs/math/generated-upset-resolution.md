# Join-indexed resolutions of generated upsets

Let `k` be a field, `P` a lattice, and `f : Fin m → P` a finite indexed family. Its generated
upset is

`U = {p : P | ∃ i, f i ≤ p}`.

For each nonempty set of occurrences `A ⊆ Fin m`, write `j_A` for the join of the values `f i`
with `i ∈ A`. The corresponding summand is the principal-upset module born at `j_A`. In degree
`r`, `TDA.Persistence.GeneratedUpsetResolution.term f r` is the finite biproduct of these
modules over the faces with `r+1` occurrences. The differential deletes each occurrence in
increasing `Fin m` order, with the alternating sign, and maps the face module into the module
of the smaller face. The augmentation maps degree zero into the support-interval module on
`U`.

The construction preserves occurrences: if two values of `f` are equal, their singleton faces
and the higher faces containing them remain distinct summands. Each term is projective because
it is isomorphic to the graded-free module on its face generators, and each principal-upset
module is projective.

At an index `p`, the active occurrences are `i` with `f i ≤ p`. The summands visible at `p`
are exactly the nonempty faces of the active-occurrence simplex. The equivalence
`termEvaluationEquiv` identifies the evaluated terms with its simplicial chains, sends the
actual differential to the signed simplicial boundary, and identifies augmentation with the
sum of degree-zero coefficients. If the active set is nonempty, coning by any active vertex
contracts the simplex chain complex, including its augmentation. If the active set is empty,
the evaluated face terms and the support-module fiber are all zero. Thus
`resolution_pointwise_exact_at` gives surjectivity and exactness at every term, while
`differential_comp_differential` and `differential_comp_augmentation` establish the chain laws.

The categorical chain complex is `chainComplex f`. Its object and differential equations
are `chainComplex_X` and `chainComplex_d`. The canonical Mathlib `projectiveResolution f`
has this complex, projective terms, and the original augmentation in degree zero, as recorded
by `projectiveResolution_complex` and `projectiveResolution_augmentation`. Pointwise exactness
and surjectivity make that augmentation a quasi-isomorphism to the complex concentrated in
degree zero at the generated-upset module.

For every lattice and finite indexed family, `generatedSupport_hasProjectiveDimensionLE`
proves Mathlib's ordinary projective-dimension bound `m−1` for the actual support module;
`generatedSupport_projectiveDimension_le` states its numerical form. No finiteness or
decidability condition on the lattice is needed. The proof uses `term_isZero_of_degree_ge`:
the degree-`r` term is zero for `m ≤ r`, since there are no faces with `r+1` occurrences.
Every Ext class in such a degree is represented by a morphism from that zero term of the
canonical resolution, so the class vanishes. This computes ordinary dimension in the
persistence-functor category.

The bound uses truncated natural subtraction. For `m = 0`, the generated support is empty
and `generatedSupport_fin_zero_module_isZero` identifies the support module as zero; its
numerical projective dimension is `⊥`. For `m = 1`, the bound gives projectivity. Two equal
labels still contribute two occurrences to the resolution, but their support equals that
of a singleton family, so their module is already projective. The occurrence-count bound
need not be sharp.

A sharp two-generator example is the upset generated by `(true, false)` and `(false, true)`
in the lattice `Bool × Bool`. Its support consists of those two atoms and their common top.
The module has one copy of the coefficient field at each supported point. It has ordinary
projective dimension exactly one over every field. To prove nonprojectivity, suppose the
free augmentation had a section. Naturality from each atom to the top would identify the
section's top coefficient vector with vectors supported on each atom's available generators.
These generator sets are disjoint, so the top vector would vanish, contradicting the section
identity on the unit coefficient. The general bound supplies the upper bound of one.

The dimension theorem is an upper bound; it does not assert that the canonical resolution is
shortest or minimal, or give a global-dimension bound. Proposition 5.1's separate strict-join condition belongs to
its shortestness conclusion and is unnecessary for the upper bound formalized here.

The construction formalizes Botnan, Oppermann, Oudot and Scoccola,
[*On the bottleneck stability of rank decompositions of multi-parameter persistence modules*,
arXiv:2208.00300v3, Proposition 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.00300v3#S5.SS1). The Lean interface is
in [`GeneratedUpsetResolution.lean`](../../TDA/Persistence/GeneratedUpsetResolution.lean) and
[`GeneratedUpsetProjectiveDimension.lean`](../../TDA/Persistence/GeneratedUpsetProjectiveDimension.lean);
focused fixtures are in [`GeneratedUpsetResolutionExamples.lean`](../../TDA/Persistence/GeneratedUpsetResolutionExamples.lean).
